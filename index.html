How it works

Traditional keyword search matches exact words. CineMatch understands meaning — so "heartwarming story about a father and son" finds The Lion King even without those words in its synopsis.

  1. 01

    Embed

    Your query is encoded into a 384‑dimensional vector by all‑MiniLM‑L6‑v2, a compact sentence‑transformer trained on hundreds of millions of text pairs. Semantic relationships become directions in this space: "space odyssey" and "interstellar voyage" land near each other, even with no shared words.

  2. 02

    Hash

    The vector is projected through 20 random hyperplanes (8 bits each). Each projection produces a short binary key — similar vectors collide into the same bucket across multiple hash tables, grouping candidate movies without looking at every embedding.

  3. 03

    Retrieve

    Only movies sharing at least one bucket with your query become candidates — typically under 1 % of the 4,803‑film index. LSH trades a small recall penalty for a massive speed-up over exhaustive nearest‑neighbor search.

  4. 04

    Rank

    Every candidate is precisely re‑scored by cosine similarity between its precomputed embedding and your query vector. The top‑K films ranked by that score are what you see — exact, not approximate.
